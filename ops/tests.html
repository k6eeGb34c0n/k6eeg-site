Audio test signals

Each hour has one audio test, in a 6-hour cycle that repeats 4 times a day, starting at 0000 UTC. Within its hour the test plays twice, at 25 and 55 minutes past the hour, in open air after the short update. Each is introduced by voice and sent at the same audio level as the original 300 to 3000 Hz sweep. See the full daily schedule.

The tests

CTCSS 100.0 Hz test

Checks that tone squelch decodes on the beacon, for possible future section tags.

Set CTCSS (tone squelch, sometimes called PL) to 100.0 Hz decode. If the announcement comes through, it decoded. Then try another tone, such as 103.5 Hz: the announcement should stay muted. The tone starts a moment before the voice so decoders have time to open.

About 20 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0000, 0600, 1200, 1800 UTC.

Audio sweep, 300 to 3000 Hz

Your receiver's audio passband in one pass: listen for where the sweep fades in and out.

Watch it on a phone spectrum or spectrogram app held to the speaker, or record the receiver audio. A flat line across the sweep means a flat audio response; FM de-emphasis rolls off the top end.

About 10 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0100, 0700, 1300, 1900 UTC.

1 kHz reference tone

A steady tone at a known level, for setting audio levels and checking deviation.

Use it to set your receiver's audio or recording level, read deviation on a service monitor, or take a SINAD reading with a SINAD meter or app.

About 10 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0200, 0800, 1400, 2000 UTC.

Stepped tones, 300 to 3000 Hz

Five tones sent at the same level, so differences in loudness are your receiver's response.

Note how loud each step sounds, or read each on a level meter app. In narrowband FM, 3000 Hz is normally several decibels below 1000 Hz because of de-emphasis.

About 11 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0300, 0900, 1500, 2100 UTC.

1 kHz level ladder

Five levels, each 6 dB below the last, from the reference level down 24 dB.

Each step should sound clearly quieter. Steps that sound the same mean something is limiting or compressing: the receiver's audio stage, a recording app's automatic gain, or (at the top) the transmitter.

About 11 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0400, 1000, 1600, 2200 UTC.

Band-limited noise, 300 to 3000 Hz

Noise covering the whole voice band at once.

On a spectrum app it draws your receiver's audio response in one look. It's faster than the sweep, and easier to compare between receivers.

About 5 seconds, after a short spoken introduction. Plays during the hours starting 0500, 1100, 1700, 2300 UTC.

Reports welcome

If you measure something interesting, such as your receiver's response, the level where a step stops changing, whether tone squelch decoded, or signal strength during a test, send it to k6eegBeacon@gmail.com with your location and equipment.
