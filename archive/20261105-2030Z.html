Maximum usable frequency 29 megahertz, measured by the Idaho Falls ionosonde via KC2G: paths within North America open through 12 meters, 10 meters marginal. Critical frequency 8.8 megahertz, so 80, 60, and 40 meters are good for NVIS. Daytime absorption limits 160 and 80 meters to short range. K index 1, very quiet. Latest solar flare: class M3.5. This is a medium-size flare, enough for an R1 radio blackout, with brief HF fadeouts on the sunlit side of Earth. Data from NOAA.

Flares black out HF on the daylight side of Earth. Try night-side paths or wait it out. Blackouts usually last minutes to an hour. Lower solar flux means the maximum usable frequency drops, so 20 meters becomes the main daytime DX band, and 40 and 80 meters do more of the work at night.
