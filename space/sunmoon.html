Tips

  • Listen first. You can hear big EME stations with a single long yagi on 2 m and a good preamp at the antenna. Weekends with a high index and a contest are the easiest time to start.
  • Digital modes carry most EME today. Q65 in WSJT-X (JT65 is still heard). On 2 m, activity centers on 144.100 to 144.150 MHz; CW EME sits near the bottom of the band.
  • Correct for Doppler. Your own echo is shifted by the amounts in the table above; on 23 cm and up it's several kilohertz. WSJT-X's Astronomical Data window tracks it.
  • Mind polarization on 2 m. Faraday rotation in the ionosphere turns the echo's polarization unpredictably; signals can fade for minutes and return. Cross-yagis or an X-pol array help. Above 1 GHz it hardly matters.
  • Moonrise and moonset are prime time for stations with fixed or low antennas: ground gain can add several dB with the moon near the horizon.
  • Avoid the sun. Within about 10 degrees of the moon it adds a lot of noise; around new moon most stations take a break.
