Tropospheric Ducting

Experimental Tropo Forecast

The beacon's tropo forecast turns NOAA's global weather model into a chance of ducting on 2 meters, 70 centimeters, and 23 centimeters, like a rain forecast. The chances were calibrated against a year of weather-balloon soundings at Oakland, San Diego, Quillayute, Lihue, and Hilo, and they check out: when the forecast says 80 percent or more, balloons found a duct over 90 percent of the time.

Region How well it works
California coast Strong: clearly separates good days from bad
Pacific Northwest coast Moderate: useful, especially on 70 cm
Hawaii Ducts are nearly always present there, so the forecast adds little

The model can say how likely ducting is, but not reliably at what height; the balloon soundings in the beacon's TROPO section give heights. For wide-area and multi-day views, see the Hepburn tropo maps and F5LEN.

The rows above give the chance of a duct at each place on its own. Lihue sits in trade-wind air where ducts are present most days, so its local chance runs high even when there is no path from California. A path toward Hawaii needs ducting at both ends and an unbroken layer across the ocean between them; that is what this row and the on-air "toward Hawaii" line report.
